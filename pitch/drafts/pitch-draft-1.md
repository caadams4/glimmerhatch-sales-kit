# Moving freight faster: how StrikeOne works with logistics teams

*Draft 1, Glimmerhatch, August 2026. Not for circulation.*

## The problem, in your words

If you run a depot, a fleet or a distribution network you already know the
three things that cost you the most: things that arrive late, things that
arrive in lumps, and things you cannot see until it is too late to move them.

None of these is a technology problem to begin with. They are scheduling and
data problems that happen to have technology wrapped round them.

## Why we work in sprints

A StrikeOne sprint is a fixed scope, delivered in two to six weeks, by a
small pod of consultants who have done it before. You agree what "done"
looks like on day one. You see something working in week two. If it is not
helping by week four, you will know, and so will we.

Logistics operations do not have spare months to wait for a strategy report.
Sprints fit the shape of the work.

## What a sprint looks like

- Week 1: look at how work really flows (we go to the depot, we do not
  just read the process document)
- Weeks 2 to 3: build and test with your own data
- Week 4: hand over, train, leave things running

## Where we usually start

1. Dock and slot scheduling
2. Route and delivery data quality
3. Billing exceptions (the invoices that bounce)
