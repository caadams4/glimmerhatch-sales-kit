# Moving freight faster

*Draft 3, Glimmerhatch, October 2026.*

## Late, lumpy, invisible

Things arrive late. Things arrive in lumps. And you find out about both
after the fact.

None of that starts as a technology problem. They are scheduling and data
problems that technology happens to sit on top of.

## Why sprints

A StrikeOne sprint has three promises:

- **Fixed scope**, agreed on day one.
- **Something working** by the end of week two.
- **A clear stop or go** at week four.


## Where we usually start

1. **Dock and slot scheduling.** Northgate Freight cut dock turnaround by
   about a fifth in four weeks.
2. **Route and delivery data.** Cleaner data at the source, fewer arguments later.
3. **Billing exceptions.** Harbourline Distribution halved the invoices
   that bounce.

And when the problem is temperature rather than time, Fenwick Cold Chain
now finds out about a breach before its customer does.

## Who you would work with

A small pod, led by an engagement manager who stays for the whole sprint.
Not a rota of strangers.

And for smaller fleets: Calder Wharf Hauliers, 25 vehicles, two weeks.
